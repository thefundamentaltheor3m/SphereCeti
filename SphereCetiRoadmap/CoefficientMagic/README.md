## Status and authority

This is a **sibling roadmap** of the roadmap in the root [`README.md`](../../README.md), which
incorporates it by reference.  It was prepared against SphereCeti

was generated by an AI system with prompting from Thomas Hales.  Its statements and arguments

paper being correct: every step it uses is a target.

`UPSTREAM.md` remain authoritative.  The original optimality and periodic-uniqueness roadmap is
unchanged by this one.

## Purpose and completion criteria

Construct the normalized magic functions by solving explicit coefficient equations, prove their
uniqueness in the entire stated coefficient space, and connect them to the existing certificate
interface. Develop the analytic and geometric explanations of those equations as reusable
mathematics.

The roadmap has four separately reported summits:

| Summit | Required mathematical result |
|---|---|
| **Independent coefficient construction** | In each dimension, the homogeneous Fourier-sample equations have a one-dimensional kernel in the specified complex `ℓ²` space; normalization gives a unique solution, with real coefficients and weighted `ℓ¹` decay. The proof uses neither existing magic functions nor Fourier interpolation. |
| **Comparison and certificate** | Identify the constructed function with the existing dimension-specific magic function, transfer its global sign inequalities, and construct the existing `CohnElkies.Certificate` with an explicit equality for its function field. |
| **Stable exact Galerkin approximation** | Construct the regularizing operator independently of the unknown solution, prove the positive equation has a bounded inverse, and prove convergence and error estimates for its exact finite-dimensional compressions. |
| **Full geometric correspondence and index** | Prove both directions of the coefficient-kernel/global-section correspondence, with all endpoint, elliptic-point, and cusp conditions, and establish the rank-six orbifold-bundle Euler characteristic calculation. |

All milestones below are required for full coverage. They need not finish simultaneously. In
particular, the finite modular candidate route can establish existence before the geometric
summit is complete. Conversely, a genuine orbifold Riemann–Roch proof can supply existence
without waiting for that alternative route.

The comparison summit inherits sign theorems from the original construction. It does not turn
the independent coefficient construction into an independent proof of the sign inequalities.

## Common core and dimension-specific data

Use one coefficient and boundary-analysis core with explicit finite dimension data. Do not
introduce a global `MagicDimension` typeclass. The two coefficient systems must remain distinct.

Let `n₀` be the first shell index and `N` its cardinality. All radial profiles use squared
radius `u = ‖x‖²`.

| Datum | Dimension 8 | Dimension 24 |
|---|---:|---:|
| First shell index `n₀` | 1 | 2 |
| First shell squared radius | 2 | 4 |
| First shell count `N` | 240 | 196560 |
| Reciprocal-integral exponent `d/2 − 2` | 2 | 10 |
| Nonpositive coefficient modes | `(0,-2), (0,0), (1,0)` | `(0,-4), (0,-2), (0,0), (1,-2), (1,0)` |
| Moment degrees imposed | All nonnegative degrees | Positive degrees |
| Normalized first-shell profile derivative | `−1/120` | `−1/65520` |
| Proposed finite modular candidate parameters/constraints | 6 / 5 | 8 / 7 |

The common positive modes are three even-index sequences and one odd-index sequence. Define the
index types explicitly and use the library's `ℓ¹` and `ℓ²` constructions, with finite-coordinate
projections and conjugation maps.

The normalizing functional is `λ(c) = c₁,₀/4`. Fourier equations require both the Fourier profile
and its squared-radius derivative to vanish at `2n` for `n ≥ n₀`. In dimension 24 the two
unprescribed zeroth moments remain real mathematical data; do not silently set them to zero.

Define `GlobalSolution d` (in Lean, `globalSolution`) analytically before introducing an orbifold: it is the vector space of
six holomorphic functions on the upper half-plane satisfying the explicit translation and
inversion matrices and the dimension-specific Laurent cusp conditions. Each condition must be
an actual equation or analytic bound. The analytic correspondence with the coefficient kernel
belongs to C3–C6; identifying this space with the genuine bundle's global sections belongs to C7.

## Milestones

### C0. Precise systems and normalization

**Owner:** Sphere-Packing-Lean, with generic sequence-space facts upstream.

Define the positive-mode functions, the removable correction functions, the coefficient index
sets, and the two sample systems. Prove the correction values and derivatives at their removable
points. Record which direct-side zeros are automatic and which first-shell derivatives remain
free. Every definition needs its characteristic evaluation equation.

Use the existing Fourier kernel `exp(-2πi⟨x,ξ⟩)` and the existing Euclidean measure. Distinguish
the paper's power-series parameter, modular q-parameter, radial variable, and coefficient mode.

**Acceptance:** small exact examples recover every finite correction jet and the two
dimension-specific index lists.

### C1. Schwartz synthesis and injectivity

**Owner:** Sphere-Packing-Lean; reusable integration and Schwartz estimates upstream.

For every coefficient vector in the specified `ℓ²` space, prove convergence of the synthesis
series in the Schwartz topology, continuity and linearity of synthesis, and injectivity. Expose
bounds that justify termwise Fourier transformation, evaluation, and differentiation.

The raw profiles are defined on `[0,∞)`. They are not automatically Schwartz functions on
`ℝ`: their displayed formulas need not decay or even converge on the negative half-line.
Construct the resulting object directly in the canonical `RadialSchwartzMap`, or prove and
identify a suitable extension before using `MagicFunction.ofNormSq`. Do not give an arbitrary
extension the status of a defining formula.

**Acceptance:** a synthesized function's value at zero equals `λ(c)`, its prescribed direct
jets vanish, and coefficient equality follows from equality of synthesized functions.

### C2. Differentiated Poisson normalization

**Owner:** shared Sphere-Packing analytic API, consuming TauCeti Poisson and shell data.

Prove a generic dilation identity for a self-dual covolume-one lattice and a radial Schwartz
function. Justify differentiating its lattice sums locally uniformly in the positive dilation
parameter. Specialize only after establishing the common theorem.

When both direct and Fourier shell values vanish, later direct-shell derivatives vanish, and all
Fourier shell derivatives vanish, obtain

\[
  \widehat f(0)=f(0),\qquad
  \Phi'(2n_0)=-\frac{d}{4n_0N}\,f(0).
\]

Thus normalization forces `−1/120` in dimension 8 and `−1/65520` in dimension 24.
Derive the corresponding exceptional coefficient identities from the correction jets.

**Acceptance:** both constants are exact corollaries of one theorem; squared-radius derivatives
are not confused with derivatives in radius.

### C3. Fourier samples, moments, and Cauchy data

**Owner:** Sphere-Packing-Lean for the concrete contours and densities; generic Cauchy tools upstream.

Derive the sample-to-moment formulas first for finite-support coefficients, then extend by the
C1 estimates. Define bounded moment operators with their exact index sets, and prove the
geometric tail estimates giving compactness.

Define the Cauchy transforms, their behavior at infinity, the jump relations on open arcs, and
the reconstruction map. Retain the zeroth-moment subtraction in dimension 24. Prove the modified
zero-constant-moment reconstruction in dimension 8.

**Acceptance:** explicit equivalence between the original Fourier equations and the specified
moment equations, including the constant rows where required. Compactness alone is not an
existence proof or an error estimate for coefficient recovery.

### C4. Endpoint continuation and coefficient decay

**Owner:** generic complex analysis in TauCeti/Mathlib; concrete endpoint geometry locally.

Prove localized trace/gluing statements, area estimates, puncture-removal criteria, and the
endpoint continuation needed by the actual contours. Keep open-arc arguments separate from
corner or endpoint claims.

First extend the four generating series to a uniform disk of some radius `R > 1`, with bounds
controlled by the coefficient norm. This gives geometric decay and `ℓ¹` membership. Apply these
properties to **every exact `ℓ²` kernel vector**, not only to a constructed modular candidate.
The stronger conclusion for every `1 < r < exp π` requires the global upper-half-plane
continuation in C6; do not attribute it to the local endpoint estimate alone.

**Acceptance:** honest domain and norm statements imply `ℓ¹` membership and support the entire
kernel's later continuation and vanishing arguments.

### C5. Existence from a finite modular family

**Owner:** Sphere-Packing-Lean, using existing scalar modular-form infrastructure.

See the companion [`FINITE_SECTION_EXISTENCE.md`](FINITE_SECTION_EXISTENCE.md) for the proposed explicit finite families.
The dimension-eight construction has six parameters subject to five homogeneous cusp
conditions; dimension 24 has eight parameters subject to seven. Exact algebraic checks support
these counts, but these checks are not Lean verification.  Only the two identities
`cuspMatrix *ᵥ witness = 0` are proved in `Suggested.lean`.

To obtain existence, prove the actual modular transformation identities, holomorphy and allowed
poles, the finite coefficient calculations, and the global-solution-to-coefficient construction.
The companion note gives explicit rational null vectors with nonzero normalization, so their
normalization can be checked directly without waiting for C6. Alternatively, prove injectivity
of the finite parameterization and use rank-nullity to obtain a nonzero admissible candidate;
on that abstract route, C6 supplies nonvanishing normalization. In either route, prove the
coefficient image has the asserted normalization and is not zero.

**Completeness of the candidate family is unnecessary for existence.** Do not make a classification
of all modular candidates a prerequisite. Conversely, the parameter count alone proves nothing
until the concrete analytic conditions and injectivity are established.

**Acceptance:** a nonzero coefficient-kernel vector constructed without either known magic
function, interpolation, or the unproved general Riemann–Roch summit.

### C6. Vanishing and independent uniqueness

**Owner:** Sphere-Packing-Lean, using generic continuation and scalar modular-form theorems.

Starting with an arbitrary `ℓ²` kernel vector whose normalization vanishes, establish the
full continuation and transformation laws needed by the vanishing proof. Apply the exact cusp
orders and the existing scalar level-one dimension/vanishing results to conclude that every
component, hence every coefficient, vanishes.

State the analytic kernel-to-`GlobalSolution` map separately, prove its inverse laws with C5's
reconstruction, and obtain the stronger weighted summability for every `1 < r < exp π` from
holomorphy of the recovered generating functions on that open disk. This analytic statement
does not assume that the orbifold or sheaf cohomology has already been formalized.

The finite candidate family's dimension does not establish uniqueness in the infinite
coefficient space. This milestone must cover the entire kernel independently.

Combine vanishing with existence to prove one-dimensionality, unique normalized existence,
reality by conjugation and uniqueness, and all normalized jets and weighted-decay conclusions.
Check declaration dependencies to substantiate the independence claim.

**Acceptance:** the first summit, with separate dimension-eight and dimension-24 theorem
statements and a common proof engine where their hypotheses coincide.

### C7. Rank-six geometry and Riemann–Roch

**Owner:** generic modular/orbifold geometry upstream; the particular bundle and cusp modification locally.

Define the rank-six holomorphic bundle with its actual transition functions and prove the
cocycle relations. Retain the elliptic stabilizers. Specify the extension at the cusp by the
concrete allowed jets, residues, and pole orders.

Identify the analytically defined `GlobalSolution d` with the bundle's actual global sections.
Compose this identification with the analytic equivalence from C3–C6 to obtain the two-way
coefficient/section correspondence, preserving its coefficient-recovery equations. Then
establish the Riemann–Roch hypotheses and the arithmetic giving Euler characteristic one
separately in dimensions 8 and 24.

The index is the bundle's cohomological Euler characteristic; it is not the index of the compact
moment operator. Scalar modular-form dimension formulas do not by themselves provide the
required orbifold vector-bundle theorem.

**Acceptance:** full geometric summit and a second existence proof. Record partial completion
of either direction explicitly; a section-to-coefficient construction alone is not the full
correspondence.

### C8. Regularization and exact Galerkin convergence

**Owner:** concrete regularizer locally; abstract operator arguments upstream.

Construct the paper's compact operator `K` from fixed Cauchy integrals, averaging, and
coefficient extraction. Prove `Kc = c` for every moment-kernel vector and the operator-norm
tail estimate. Its definition must not use the unknown normalized solution.

For the moment map `A`, define

\[
  L=(I-K)^*(I-K)+A^*A,\qquad N=L+\lambda^*\lambda.
\]

Prove positivity, self-adjointness, identity-plus-compact structure, the kernel identity,
coercivity, bounded invertibility, and
`c* = N⁻¹ λ*(1)`. Apply the existing compact-estimate and finite-section APIs.

Define finite projections retaining every exceptional coordinate. Prove each exact compression
has the same coercivity lower bound and establish

\[
 \|c_J-c_*\|_2
 \leq \frac{\|N\|}{c_{\rm coer}}\|(I-P_J)c_*\|_2.
\]

**Acceptance:** exact Galerkin convergence with the geometric tails obtained in C4 and C6. A merely
existential positive coercivity constant does not certify floating-point matrices or their
computed solutions.

### C9. Comparison and certificate handoff

**Owner:** Sphere-Packing-Lean, sharing original roadmap Layer 9.

Express each existing magic function in the new coefficient family, prove admissibility and the
sample equations, and invoke C6. Check constants against the original roadmap's adopted contour
and Fourier conventions, including its E8 sign correction.

Transfer real-valuedness, direct nonpositivity, Fourier nonnegativity, exact zeros, and local
zero-order statements with their existing scopes. Leech's direct-side exact-zero theorem remains
restricted to `‖x‖ ≥ 2`.

Construct the existing certificate through `Certificate.ofRadial`, prove its function equals
the new synthesis, and prove normalization. The original density and rigidity theorems then
consume it unchanged.

**Acceptance:** the comparison/certificate summit with an explicit dependency on the earlier
sign proofs, distinct from the independent-construction summit.

geometry into separately useful targets. Do not make the production construction wait for
unrelated lattice classification or universal-optimality infrastructure.

claimed by these summits.
