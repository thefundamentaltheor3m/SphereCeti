## Status and authority

This is a sibling roadmap of the roadmap in the root [`README.md`](../../README.md), which
incorporates it by reference.  It was prepared against SphereCeti
[`bab18933`](https://github.com/thefundamentaltheor3m/SphereCeti/tree/bab18933b567e33d1016816aca2e6a41db42ca43).

type-checks, with the proofs omitted.  It asserts no new theorem as proved.

The mathematical source is [*A construction of the sphere-packing magic functions in dimensions
8 and 24*, Hexagon 2610.00330v1](https://hexagonmath.org/2610.00330v1).  The paper states that it
was generated by an AI system with prompting from Thomas Hales.  Its statements and arguments
are material to verify.  They are not imported formal theorems, and nothing here relies on the
paper being correct: every step it uses is a target.

The root `CONVENTIONS.md`, the root roadmap's division of work between this repository and
production, the pinned dependencies, and the single `UPSTREAM.md` remain authoritative.  The

## Reading this roadmap

The conventions of the root [`README.md`](../../README.md) apply.  This roadmap also uses the
following terms.

- **Coefficient vector, mode.**  A coefficient vector `c` has entries `c_{j,m}` indexed by modes
  `(j, m)`: the positive modes, with `m > 0` even for `j = 0, 1, 2` and odd for `j = 3`, and a
  finite dimension-specific set of nonpositive modes.  Coefficient vectors form the space `ℓ²`
  of this index set, and `λ(c) = c₁,₀/4` is the normalizing functional.
- **Squared-radius profile.**  The function `Φ_c(u)` of `u = ‖x‖²` given by the series of the
  `c_{j,m}` times explicit functions of `u`; the synthesized radial function is
  `x ↦ Φ_c(‖x‖²)`.  It is specified only on the half-line `u ≥ 0`, since the series need not
  converge for `u < 0`.
- **Sample equations.**  The homogeneous linear equations saying that the squared-radius profile
  `G_c` of the Fourier transform and its derivative vanish at `2n` for every `n ≥ n₀`.
- **Moment operator.**  The bounded operator `M_d` from coefficient vectors to `ℓ²` whose
  entries are `8π G_c(2n)` and `−8 G_c'(2n)` for `n ≥ n₀`; by the paper, these are moments of
  two contour measures `μ₀, μ₁`.
- **Kernel.**  Here *the kernel* is the null space of the moment operator, that is, the solution
  space of the sample equations (Lean: `sampleKernel`); it is unrelated to the root guide's
  *kernels*, the integrands of contour integrals.  For a matrix we say *null space*.
- **Global analytic solution.**  A six-tuple of holomorphic functions on the upper half-plane
  satisfying translation and inversion laws given by explicit `6 × 6` matrices and
  dimension-specific bounds on its orders at the cusp; they form the space `GlobalSolution d`
  (Lean: `globalSolution`).  A *section* is always a section of the rank-six bundle of C7.
- **Cusp coordinate and series argument.**  The cusp is the cusp `i∞` of the modular group.
  Laurent expansions there are in `𝔮 = exp(2πiτ)`; the coefficient generating series are
  evaluated at `q = e^π exp(πiτ)`, so `q² = e^{2π} 𝔮`.
- **Euler characteristic.**  `χ(𝓔_d) = h⁰(𝓔_d) − h¹(𝓔_d)` for the rank-six orbifold bundle
  `𝓔_d` of C7.  It is never called an index.
- **Stated only in prose.**  A target described here that has no Lean declaration yet, often
  because its definition needs contour or boundary-trace data not yet fixed in Lean.
- **Explicit null vector; solution.**  C5 builds six-tuples of functions depending linearly on
  6 (`d = 8`) or 8 (`d = 24`) parameters, from explicit weakly holomorphic modular forms and
  their derivatives.  The cusp conditions are linear in the parameters, with a rational matrix,
  and an *explicit null vector* is a rational vector annihilated by that matrix.  The word
  *solution* is used only for a solution of a named system: a *global analytic solution*, or a
  solution of the sample equations.  That a null vector gives a global analytic solution, and
  that a global analytic solution gives a solution of the sample equations, are separate
  targets.

## Purpose and completion criteria

Construct the normalized magic functions by solving explicit coefficient equations, prove their
uniqueness in the entire stated coefficient space, and connect them to the existing Cohn–Elkies
certificate.  Develop the analytic and geometric explanations of those equations in a
generality useful beyond this application.

The roadmap has four main results, each reported separately:

| Main result | Required mathematical result |
|---|---|
| **Coefficient theorem** | In each dimension, the homogeneous Fourier-sample equations have a one-dimensional kernel in the specified complex `ℓ²` space; the normalization `λ(c) = 1` gives a unique solution, with real coefficients and weighted `ℓ¹` decay. The proof uses neither existing magic functions nor Fourier interpolation. |
| **Comparison** | Identify the constructed function with the existing dimension-specific magic function, transfer its global sign inequalities, and construct a `CohnElkies.Certificate` whose function equals that of `E8.certificate` / `Leech.certificate`. |
| **Regularized equation** | Construct the regularizing operator independently of the unknown solution, prove that the positive operator `N` of C8 has a bounded inverse, and prove convergence and error estimates for the *Galerkin approximations with exactly computed entries*: the solutions of the finite sections `P_J N P_J` whose matrix entries are computed exactly, not numerically approximated. |
| **Geometric correspondence** | Identify the space of global analytic solutions with the space `H⁰(𝓔_d)` of global sections of the rank-six orbifold bundle `𝓔_d`, with all elliptic-point and cusp conditions, and compute the Euler characteristic of `𝓔_d`; with C6 this gives the correspondence in both directions between the kernel and `H⁰(𝓔_d)`. |

All milestones below are required for full coverage, but they need not be completed together.
In particular, the finite-dimensional family of modular forms of C5, with the explicit null
vectors of its cusp matrices, can give existence (together with the analytic targets of C1, C3
and C5) before the geometric correspondence is complete; conversely, an orbifold Riemann–Roch
proof gives existence without C5.

The comparison takes the sign inequalities from the original construction; the
coefficient construction does not give an independent proof of them.

## Common core and dimension-specific data

Develop the coefficient systems and the analysis of boundary values on the contours once, with
the explicit data of each dimension as parameters.  Do not introduce a global `MagicDimension`
typeclass.  The two coefficient systems must remain distinct.

Let `n₀` be such that the first shell has squared norm `2n₀`, and let `N₁` be its cardinality.
All radial profiles are functions of the squared radius `u = ‖x‖²`.

| Datum | Dimension 8 | Dimension 24 |
|---|---:|---:|
| `n₀` | 1 | 2 |
| First shell squared radius `2n₀` | 2 | 4 |
| First shell count `N₁` | 240 | 196560 |
| Reciprocal-integral exponent `d/2 − 2` | 2 | 10 |
| Nonpositive coefficient modes | `(0,-2), (0,0), (1,0)` | `(0,-4), (0,-2), (0,0), (1,-2), (1,0)` |
| Moment degrees imposed | All nonnegative degrees | Positive degrees |
| Normalized first-shell profile derivative | `−1/120` | `−1/65520` |
| Parameters / conditions of the finite-dimensional family (C5) | 6 / 5 | 8 / 7 |

The positive modes, common to both dimensions, form three sequences with `m` even and one with `m`
odd.  Define the index sets explicitly and use Mathlib's `ℓ¹` and `ℓ²` spaces, with the
coordinate projections onto finite sets of modes and the conjugation maps.

The normalizing functional is `λ(c) = c₁,₀/4`. The sample equations require both the Fourier
profile and its squared-radius derivative to vanish at `2n` for `n ≥ n₀`. In dimension 24 the
two zeroth moments are not prescribed; they are unknowns of the problem and must not be set to
zero.

The space `GlobalSolution d` (in Lean, `globalSolution`) of global analytic solutions is defined
analytically, without an orbifold: it is the vector space of six-tuples of holomorphic functions
on the upper half-plane satisfying the translation and inversion laws given by explicit matrices
and the dimension-specific Laurent conditions at the cusp.  Each condition is an equation or an
analytic bound.  The correspondence between this space and the kernel belongs to C3–C6; its
identification with the space `H⁰(𝓔_d)` of global sections of the bundle belongs to C7.

## Milestones

### C0. Precise systems and normalization

**Home:** Sphere-Packing-Lean, with general facts about sequence spaces upstream.

Define the positive-mode functions, the removable correction functions, the coefficient index
sets, and the two sample systems. Prove the correction values and derivatives at their removable
points. Record which direct-side zeros are automatic and which first-shell derivatives remain
free. Each definition comes with an equation that determines its values.

Use the existing Fourier convention, with phase `exp(-2πi⟨x,ξ⟩)`, and the existing Euclidean
measure.  Keep
distinct the argument `q` of the generating series, the cusp coordinate `𝔮`, the radial
variable, and the coefficient mode.

**Acceptance:** small examples, computed exactly, recover every finite correction jet and the two
dimension-specific index sets.

### C1. Schwartz synthesis and injectivity

**Home:** Sphere-Packing-Lean; general integration and Schwartz estimates upstream.

For every coefficient vector in the specified `ℓ²` space, prove convergence of the synthesis
series in the Schwartz topology, continuity and linearity of synthesis, and injectivity.  State
bounds that justify termwise Fourier transformation, evaluation, and differentiation.

The profiles are defined on `[0,∞)`.  They are not automatically Schwartz functions on
`ℝ`: their displayed formulas need not decay or even converge on the negative half-line.
Construct the resulting object directly in the canonical `RadialSchwartzMap`, or prove that a
suitable extension exists and identify it before using `MagicFunction.ofNormSq`.  An arbitrary
extension must not be treated as a defining formula.

**Acceptance:** a synthesized function's value at zero equals `λ(c)`, its prescribed direct
jets vanish, and coefficient equality follows from equality of synthesized functions.

### C2. Differentiated Poisson summation

**Home:** the analytic results shared with the root roadmap in Sphere-Packing-Lean, using
TauCeti's Poisson summation and shell data.

Prove a general dilation identity for a self-dual covolume-one lattice and a radial Schwartz
function. Justify differentiating its lattice sums locally uniformly in the positive dilation
parameter. Specialize only after proving the general theorem.

When both direct and Fourier shell values vanish, later direct-shell derivatives vanish, and all
Fourier shell derivatives vanish, obtain

\[
  \widehat f(0)=f(0),\qquad
  \Phi'(2n_0)=-\frac{d}{4n_0N_1}\,f(0).
\]

Thus the normalization `λ(c) = f(0) = 1` forces `Φ'(2n₀) = −1/120` in dimension 8 and
`−1/65520` in dimension 24.  Derive from the correction jets the corresponding identities for
the exceptional coefficient `c_{0,−2n₀}`.

**Acceptance:** both constants follow from one theorem; derivatives in the squared radius are
kept distinct from derivatives in the radius.

### C3. Fourier samples, moments, and Cauchy data

**Home:** Sphere-Packing-Lean for the concrete contours and densities; general Cauchy-transform
results upstream.

Derive the sample-to-moment formulas first for finitely supported coefficient vectors, then
extend by the C1 estimates. Define the bounded moment operators with their index sets stated
exactly, and prove the geometric tail estimates giving compactness.

Define the Cauchy transforms, their behavior at infinity, the jump relations on open arcs, and
the reconstruction map.  In dimension 24, keep the subtraction of the zeroth moments; in
dimension 8, prove the modified reconstruction in which the zeroth (constant) moments vanish.

**Acceptance:** an explicit equivalence between the Fourier-sample equations and the specified
moment equations, including the constant rows where required.  Compactness alone gives neither
existence nor an error estimate for coefficient recovery.

### C4. Endpoint continuation and coefficient decay

**Home:** general complex analysis in TauCeti or Mathlib; the geometry of the particular
endpoints in production.

Prove local trace and gluing statements, bounds for area integrals, criteria for removing
punctures, and the continuation at the endpoints required by the contours of the paper.  Keep
arguments on open arcs separate from statements at corners or endpoints.

First extend the four generating series to a disk of some radius `R > 1` independent of the
coefficient vector, with bounds controlled by the coefficient norm.  This gives geometric decay
and membership in `ℓ¹` for every kernel vector in `ℓ²`, not only for one constructed from
modular forms.  The stronger conclusion for every `1 < r < exp π` requires the continuation to
the whole upper half-plane in C6 and does not follow from the local estimate at the endpoints.

**Acceptance:** statements with explicit domains and norms imply membership in `ℓ¹` and suffice
for the later continuation and vanishing arguments for the whole kernel.

### C5. Existence from a finite-dimensional family of modular forms

**Home:** Sphere-Packing-Lean, using the existing results on scalar modular forms.

The companion note [`MODULAR_EXISTENCE.md`](MODULAR_EXISTENCE.md) gives the
proposed finite-dimensional families explicitly.  In dimension 8 the family has six parameters
subject to five homogeneous conditions at the cusp; in dimension 24 it has eight parameters
subject to seven.  The script checks the matrices of these conditions, their ranks, and the
null vectors; these are computational checks, not Lean proofs.  Among the C5 statements, only
the two rational identities `cuspMatrix *ᵥ witness = 0` are proved in Lean; they involve no
modular forms.

To obtain existence, prove the theta and modular transformation identities, holomorphy and the
permitted poles, the identification of each row of the cusp matrix with a Laurent coefficient
and the implication from the vanishing of those coefficients to the cusp-order bounds, and the
construction of a coefficient vector from a global analytic solution (which needs C1 and the
sample-to-moment identity of C3).  The companion note gives explicit rational null vectors; the
normalizations of the coefficient vectors of the corresponding global analytic solutions can be
computed directly (the script gives `180π` and `98280π`), independently of C6.  Alternatively, prove
injectivity of the parametrization and use rank–nullity to obtain a nonzero parameter vector
annihilated by the cusp matrix; in that argument, C6 shows that the normalization of the
resulting coefficient vector is nonzero.  In either case, prove that the resulting coefficient
vector has nonzero normalization, and rescale it to normalization 1.

Existence does not require the family to contain every global analytic solution, and a
classification of all such solutions is not a prerequisite.  The parameter count alone proves
nothing until the analytic conditions and injectivity are established.

**Acceptance:** a kernel vector with normalization 1, constructed without either known magic
function, Fourier interpolation, or the as yet unproved general Riemann–Roch argument of the
geometric correspondence.

### C6. Vanishing and independent uniqueness

**Home:** Sphere-Packing-Lean, using general continuation results and theorems on scalar modular
forms.

For an arbitrary kernel vector in `ℓ²` whose normalization vanishes, prove the full continuation
and the transformation laws needed by the vanishing argument.  The cusp-order bounds and the
existing results on scalar modular forms of level one (dimension and vanishing) then show that
every component, hence every coefficient, vanishes.

State the analytic map from the kernel to `GlobalSolution d` separately, prove that it and the
reconstruction of C5 are mutually inverse, and deduce the stronger weighted summability for every
`1 < r < exp π` from holomorphy of the recovered generating functions on `|q| < exp π`.  This
analytic statement does not assume that the orbifold or sheaf cohomology has already been
formalized.

The dimension count in the finite-dimensional family does not establish uniqueness in the
infinite-dimensional coefficient space; this milestone treats the entire kernel independently.

Vanishing and existence together give one-dimensionality of the kernel, existence and uniqueness
of the normalized solution, its reality (by conjugation and uniqueness), the values and
first-shell derivatives of the normalized function, and the weighted decay.  The claim of
independence is to be checked from the dependencies of the declarations.

**Acceptance:** the coefficient theorem, with separate theorems in dimensions 8 and 24 and a
common argument where their hypotheses coincide.

### C7. Rank-six geometry and Riemann–Roch

**Home:** the general geometry of modular curves and orbifolds upstream; the particular bundle
and its modification at the cusp in production.

Define the rank-six holomorphic bundle by its transition functions and prove the cocycle
relations, keeping the stabilizers of the elliptic points.  Specify the extension over the cusp
by the permitted jets, residues, and pole orders.

Identify `GlobalSolution d` with the space `H⁰(𝓔_d)` of global sections of the bundle `𝓔_d`.
Composed with the
analytic equivalence of C3–C6, this gives the correspondence in both directions between kernel
vectors and sections, compatible with the equations recovering the coefficients.  Then verify the
hypotheses of Riemann–Roch and compute that the Euler characteristic is one, separately in
dimensions 8 and 24.

Dimension formulas for scalar modular forms do not by themselves provide the required theorem
for orbifold vector bundles.

**Acceptance:** the geometric correspondence and a second proof of existence.  Partial completion of
either direction is recorded explicitly; a construction of coefficients from sections alone is
not the full correspondence.

### C8. Regularization and Galerkin convergence

**Home:** the concrete regularizer in production; the abstract operator theory upstream.

Construct the paper's compact operator `K` from fixed Cauchy integrals, averaging, and
coefficient extraction.  Prove `Kc = c` for every vector in the kernel of the moment operator,
and the tail estimate in operator norm.  The definition of `K` must not use the unknown
normalized solution.

For bounded operators `K`, `A` and the functional `λ` (applied with `A = M_d`), define

\[
  L=(I-K)^*(I-K)+A^*A,\qquad N=L+\lambda^*\lambda.
\]

Prove positivity, self-adjointness, the decomposition as identity plus compact, the identity `ker
L = ker(I − K) ∩ ker A` (which is `ker M_d`, since `K` fixes `ker M_d`), coercivity, bounded
invertibility, and `c_* = N⁻¹ λ^*(1)`.  Use TauCeti's compact-estimate and finite-section results
listed above.

Define the coordinate projections `P_J` that keep every nonpositive mode and the positive modes
with `m ≤ J`.  Prove that each finite section `P_J N P_J` with exactly computed entries has the
same coercivity lower bound `c_coer` and establish, for the solution `c_J` of the finite section,

\[
 \|c_J-c_*\|_2
 \leq \frac{\|N\|}{c_{\rm coer}}\|(I-P_J)c_*\|_2.
\]

**Acceptance:** convergence of the Galerkin approximations, with the geometric tails
obtained in C4 and C6.  A positive coercivity constant that is only known to exist does not
justify floating-point matrices or their computed solutions.

### C9. Comparison and the certificate

**Home:** Sphere-Packing-Lean, together with Layer 9 of the original roadmap.

Express each existing magic function in the new coefficient family, prove that its coefficient
vector lies in `ℓ²` and satisfies the sample equations, and apply C6.  Check constants against
the original roadmap's adopted contour and Fourier conventions, including its E8 sign correction.

Transfer real-valuedness, nonpositivity on the direct side, nonnegativity on the Fourier side,
zero sets, and local statements on orders of zeros, each with its existing scope.  Leech's
direct-side zero-set theorem remains restricted to `‖x‖ ≥ 2`.

Construct through `Certificate.ofRadial` a `CohnElkies.Certificate` whose function is the new
synthesis and equals that of `E8.certificate` / `Leech.certificate`, and prove its
normalization.  The original density and rigidity theorems then apply to it unchanged.

**Acceptance:** the comparison, with an explicit dependency on the earlier sign proofs, kept
distinct from the coefficient theorem.

   jets in closed form; and, for C5, `V₂, W₄` from the existing `H₂, H₄`, with shared `S,T`
   transformations and `det[V₂,W₄]² = 576Δ`, using `jacobi_identity` and `Δ_eq_H₂_H₃_H₄`.
2. General differentiated Poisson identity and the two first-shell constants.
3. Positive-mode estimates and continuous Schwartz synthesis.
4. Synthesis injectivity and Fourier-sample continuity.
5. Sample-to-moment identities, with the constant rows treated separately.
6. The finite-dimensional family of C5, with proofs, and its matrix of cusp conditions; its
   algebra and its lemmas on initial Laurent coefficients in `𝔮` can precede the analysis of
   boundary values on the contours.
7. Existence by passing from a global analytic solution to coefficients; then continuation at the
   endpoints and for the entire kernel.
8. Vanishing of kernel vectors with zero normalization, and the coefficient theorem.
9. Regularizer, inverse of the positive operator, and the Galerkin approximations with exactly
   computed entries.
10. Comparison and the certificate.

C7 may proceed in parallel once its transition functions and cusp data are fixed; its general
geometry should be split into targets that are useful on their own.  The production construction
does not depend on lattice classification or on universal optimality.

## Repository integration and review

This roadmap is registered with the repository.  The root README
incorporates this specification; `sphereceti.toml` registers this document, the companion note,
and the three target files under `[[sources.additional_roadmaps]]`; the review-context
selection and the static checker cover them; and the compiled admission ledger in
`policy/audits.json` and `VALIDATION.md` include their declarations.  A linked document that is
not registered is not approved review context.

The imports are acyclic: `Suggested.lean` uses the shared foundations of the original targets;
`Comparison.lean` and `Geometry.lean` import it; the roadmap root aggregates all of them.  If
shared statements later need extraction from the original target file, perform a
semantics-preserving extraction with compatibility names.

The frozen `Pinned.lean` is untouched.  Intended upstream homes are recorded in the existing
`UPSTREAM.md`, exact mathematical sources in `PROVENANCE.md`, and the new normalization choices
in `CONVENTIONS.md`.  Proofs are to be written in the established production repositories.

Review must distinguish plausible arguments of the source, exact algebra checked outside Lean,
target statements checked by Lean, and complete proofs free of admissions.  Each constructed
object needs a concrete equation or equivalence; a missing hypothesis must never be hidden in an
unconstrained `Prop` field.

An independent proof of the global sign inequalities, rigorous numerical evaluation, Fourier
interpolation, universal optimality, and quantitative packing stability are separate future
extensions.  None of them is claimed by these main results.
